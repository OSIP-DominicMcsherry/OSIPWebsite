Where it shows up

Five areas of a life

None of them stands alone. When one slips, the others usually feel it.

  • Body

    Sleep, movement, food, energy and pain. Looking after the place you actually live, rather than trying to perfect it.

  • Mind

    Your thoughts, moods and the stories you tell yourself. Learning to notice them instead of automatically obeying them.

  • Relationships

    Partners, family, friends and the way you treat yourself. Whether anyone actually knows what's going on with you.

  • Environment

    Your home, your routines, your phone and the company you keep. What surrounds you quietly shapes everything else.

  • Purpose

    Work, direction and what you give yourself to. Whether you chose where you are, or just arrived there.

  • What is happening?

How OSIP looks at it

The five Foundations

Five ways of looking at the same life. Not rules, and not a checklist.

  1. Remain a Student

    Stay curious enough to be wrong, about yourself, about life, and about OSIP too. Confidence can live alongside doubt; certainty that arrives fast usually hasn't been checked.

  2. Begin with Reality

    Change starts from what's actually happening, not the version you've edited for comfort. Seeing something clearly isn't accepting it. It just lets you respond to the real thing.

  3. Recognise What Is Yours to Carry

    Your actions, words, choices and boundaries are yours. Other people's opinions and the past aren't. Getting precise about that line stops you spending everything on what was never yours to fix.

  4. Nothing Stands Alone

    Sleep affects mood, mood affects relationships, relationships affect motivation. Pull one thread and the rest moves, so the real lever is often somewhere other than where the problem shows up.

  5. Human Before Perfect

    You'll slip, stall and get things wrong. That's being a person, not proof you're broken. Responsibility doesn't need cruelty: treat yourself like a human being while you change.
